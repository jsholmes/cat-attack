# A 10-minute 'creative director' meeting with Kit

**Bring:** His original paper, two pencils, this preliminary brief, and the agent's `output/KIT_ONE_PAGE.md` after Phase 1.

Start with: *“You made Dragon Killers. I've got a helper who's trying to understand your drawings. Can you tell us which ideas it got right and which ones it got wrong?”*

Let him tell the story by pointing to his art. Ask **only a few** of these; skip whatever the agent has already confidently resolved:

1. **“Show me Cat Attack's coolest move. What does the player do to make it happen?”** (Q2)
2. **“What are these little things you drew? What happens when Cat Attack grabs one?”** (Q3)
3. **“What happens at your castle? Is that where the bosses live—or something way different?”** (Q4/Q10)
4. **“When Cat Attack turns gigantic, what can he do that he couldn't do before?”** (Q6–Q7)
5. **“Tell me the story of this enemy picture. What happened to YOU here?”** (Q5/Q8/Q10)

Finish with one small production choice: *“Which ONE thing should we make move first: Cat Attack, a bad guy, the giant power or the castle?”*

**Good producer habits**
- Reflect his words: *“So you're saying the castle turns into a trap?”* rather than *“Maybe the castle is a boss arena?”*
- When he disagrees with a generated image or interpretation, say: *“Great catch, we'll change it.”*
- Write his exact words into the canon and date the decision. Where two ideas conflict, ask which is latest rather than treating one as wrong.
- Keep visible evidence of his authorship: early sketches, voice notes if he agrees, versions of his designs, and change requests.
- Avoid praising everything indiscriminately; respond with real curiosity: *“That will be hard to program—how should it work?”*

The entire session can end once he has clarified the most important thing. You can do another later.
